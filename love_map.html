📊 Interpreting your score

10 or higher: This is an area of strength. You have a fairly detailed map of your partner's everyday life, hopes, fears, and dreams — you know what makes them "tick." Try not to take this knowledge for granted. Keeping in touch this way ensures you'll be well equipped to handle any challenges that arise.

Below 10: Your relationship could stand some improvement in this area. Perhaps you never had the time or tools to really get to know each other deeply, or your love maps have become outdated as your lives have changed. By taking the time to learn more about your partner now, you'll find your relationship grows stronger.

Exercise 1: The Love Map 20 Questions Game

A fun, playful way to discover how well you know each other — and to learn more.

From The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work — Chapter 1: Principle One

📖 Instructions

Play this game together in the spirit of laughter and gentle fun. The more you play, the more you'll learn about the love maps concept and how to apply it to your own relationship.

Step 1. Press Generate 20 Random Questions below. This picks 20 questions at random from the full list of 60.

Step 2. Take turns asking each other the questions. If your partner answers correctly (you be the judge!), they receive the points shown for that question — and you receive 1 bonus point for asking. If they answer incorrectly, neither of you scores.

Step 3. Use the scoreboard to track points. The person with the higher score after all 20 questions wins!

💡 Play this as frequently as you'd like. Each game is a fresh set of 20 questions.

Exercise 2: Asking Open-Ended Questions

Go deeper — update your love map with questions that invite stories, feelings, and meaning.

From The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work — Chapter 1: Principle One

📖 Instructions

Updating your love map is as important as the map itself. The process entails asking open-ended questions, and then remembering the answers. An open-ended question is one that can't be answered with a quick "yes" or "no" — instead, it invites your partner to offer up their experiences, opinions, and emotions. Asking one demonstrates genuine interest in your partner's inner world.

Choose four to ask each other. Switch off being the speaker and the listener. After your partner answers your question, follow up with an open-ended question of your own — then answer the original question you asked your partner. Take turns.

Of course, you don't have to limit yourselves to just four. Over time, you can go through all of them — it can be entertaining and enlightening. You don't have to complete them in one sitting.

Exercise 3: Who Am I?

A deep self-exploration to share with your partner — your triumphs, wounds, emotions, and dreams.

From The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work — Chapter 1: Principle One

📖 Instructions

The more you know about each other's inner world, the more profound and rewarding your relationship will be. This exercise is designed both to guide you through self-exploration and to help you share that exploration with your partner. Work on this even if you consider yourselves open books — there's always more to know, and life changes us.

Find time and privacy. Many of these questions are powerful. Reserve this for an uninterrupted stretch when you have no deadlines or distractions. Most likely you won't complete it in one sitting — that's okay. Break it up by section and proceed slowly, together.

Answer as candidly as you can. Write your thoughts in the text areas below (or in a private journal). When you're ready, share with each other what you've written and discuss what this added knowledge means for your relationship.

🏅 My Triumphs & Strivings

Reflect on your psychological triumphs — experiences where you came through trials and tribulations even better off.

Prompt 1
What has happened in your life that you are particularly proud of? Write about your psychological triumphs, experiences that exceeded your expectations, periods when you came through trials and tribulations even better off.
Prompt 2
How have these successes shaped your life? How have they affected the way you think of yourself and your capabilities? How have they influenced your goals and the things you strive for?
Prompt 3
What role has pride — feeling proud, being praised, expressing praise for others — played in your life? Did your parents show you they were proud of you as a child? How?
Prompt 4
How have other people responded to your accomplishments? Did your parents show that they loved you? Was affection readily expressed in your family? If not, what are the effects of this for your relationship today?
Prompt 5
What role does pride in your accomplishments play in your relationship? What role do your own strivings have in your relationship? What do you want your partner to know and understand about these aspects of yourself — your past, present, and plans for the future? How do you show pride in each other?
🌱 Injuries & Healing

Explore the difficult experiences you've carried — and the remarkable ways you've survived and grown from them.

Prompt 1
What difficult events or periods have you gone through? Write about any significant psychological injuries, losses, disappointments, trials, and tribulations — including periods of stress, hopelessness, and loneliness. Also include any deep traumas you have undergone as a child or adult.
Prompt 2
How have you survived these traumas? What are their lasting effects on you?
Prompt 3
How did you strengthen and heal yourself? How did you redress your grievances? How did you revive and restore yourself?
Prompt 4
How did you protect yourself against this ever happening again?
Prompt 5
How do these injuries — and the ways you protect and heal yourself — affect your relationship today? What do you want your partner to know and understand about these aspects of yourself?
💜 My Emotional World

Understanding where our emotional patterns come from helps us bring more awareness to how we connect.

Prompt 1
How did your family express the following emotions when you were a child? Reflect on each: Anger · Sadness · Fear · Affection · Interest in one another · Pride in one another
Prompt 2
During your childhood, did your family have to cope with a particular emotional challenge — such as conflict between parents, a depressed parent, or an emotionally wounded one? What implications does this have for your relationship and other close relationships today?
Prompt 3
What is your own philosophy about expressing feelings — particularly sadness, anger, fear, pride, and love? Are any of these difficult for you to express, or to hear your partner express? What is the basis of this perspective?
Prompt 4
What differences exist between you and your partner in the area of expressing emotion? What is behind these differences? What are the implications of these differences for your relationship?
✨ My Mission & Legacy

What are you here for? What do you want to leave behind? These are among the most meaningful questions you can ask yourself.

Prompt 1
Imagine you are standing in a graveyard looking at your own tombstone. Write the epitaph you would like to see there. Begin with the words: "Here lies…"
Prompt 2
Write your own obituary. (It does not have to be brief.) How do you want people to think of your life — how do you want to be remembered?
Prompt 3
Write a mission statement for your own life. What is the purpose of your life? What is its meaning? What are you trying to accomplish? What is your larger struggle?
Prompt 4
What legacy would you like to leave when you die?
Prompt 5
What significant goals have you yet to realize? This can be creating something, having a particular experience, or any aspiration — big or small.
🌸 Who I Want to Become

We are all involved in becoming the person we most want to be. Take a moment to reflect on that journey.

Prompt 1
Describe the person you want to become.
Prompt 2
How can you best help yourself become that person?
Prompt 3
What struggles have you already faced in trying to become that person?
Prompt 4
What demons in yourself have you had to fight — or still have to fight?
Prompt 5
What would you most like to change about yourself?
Prompt 6
What dreams have you denied yourself or failed to develop?
Prompt 7
What do you want your life to be like in five years?
Prompt 8
What is the story of the kind of person you would like to be?
